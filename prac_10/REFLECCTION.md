## Estimates

Regarding the **estimates** that you did for practical tasks...

### How was your estimate accuracy usually?

At the beginning of the subject, my estimates were often inaccurate.  
I tended to underestimate the time required, especially for practicals that involved debugging or learning new concepts.  
Most of the time, I would expect a task to take 1–2 hours, but it often ended up taking twice as long.

### How did your estimate accuracy improve or change during the course of the subject?

As the weeks went on, my estimates slowly became more realistic.  
I learned to consider not only the coding part, but also reading instructions, fixing mistakes, testing properly, and writing documentation.  
By the end of the subject, my estimates were much closer to the actual time, and I became more aware of my working pace.

### What did you learn from doing these estimates?

I learned that estimating is a skill that improves through practice.  
It also helped me understand where my time actually goes and encouraged me to plan ahead instead of rushing near deadlines.  
Overall, it made me more disciplined and aware of how much effort certain tasks require.

## Code Reviews

### What have you learned from being reviewed by other people?

Being reviewed by other people helped me notice things I would normally overlook—such as naming consistency, unnecessary complexity, and missing edge-case handling.  
It also made me realise that clear code is not just about “making it work,” but making sure someone else can read and understand it easily.

### What have you learned from doing code reviews of other people?

Doing code reviews taught me to think more critically about code structure.  
It pushed me to analyse logic flow, readability, and potential improvements rather than focusing only on correctness.  
Reviewing others made me more conscious of my own coding habits, and I became more consistent as a result.
